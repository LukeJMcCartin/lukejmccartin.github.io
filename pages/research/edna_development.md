Over the past decade, sequencing and quantifying nucleic acids (eDNA and eRNA) from environmental samples has been rapidly adopted as an economical approach for biodiversity assessment. Using this approach, from a small volume of seawater or sediment, the biodiversity of animals in an aquatic habitat can be non-invasively and efficiently assessed. While eDNA analysis is powerful, how detections and concentrations of nucleic acids are accurately related to biodiversity is an area of active debate and research. My PhD and postdoctoral research have determined the environmental factors that govern the degradation and spatial distribution of eDNA from marine animals. Further pressing questions must be answered before eDNA technologies can be widely adopted into a natural resource management framework.

  <p>Together with colleagues at Lehigh University and the Smithsonian National Museum of Natural History, I generated low coverage shallow genome "skims" from marine invertebrates collected in Puerto Rico. I assembled DNA reference barcodes from these genomic data rather than through conventional PCR amplification/Sanger sequencing. These data allow us to account for both intragenomic and intergenomic variation in DNA barcodes, improving the exploratory power of eDNA in the deep sea.</p>

  <p>Read about these projects in publications in <a href="https://doi.org/10.1111/1755-0998.70130">Molecular Ecology Resources</a> and <a href="https://doi.org/10.1002/ece3.11254">Methods in Ecology and Evolution</a>.</p>

  <p>One of the main accomplishments of my dissertation research was to develop a new strategy for sequencing eDNA from corals. To do this, I designed primers that amplify the 28S gene in all anthozoan coral taxa: stony corals (reef-forming),black corals, and octocorals. Together with then intern and now master's student Annemarie Wood at the Smithsonian, we also found that this method is effective for gelatinous zooplankton - a notoriously hard to sample group.</p>

  <p>Read about these projects in publications in <a href="https://doi.org/10.7717/peerj.18607">PeerJ</a> and <a href="https://doi.org/10.3389/fmars.2025.1535149">Frontier's in Marine Science</a>.</p>
