Our ability to conduct ecosystem-based conservation and restoration in aquatic and terrestrial ecosystems is hindered by extensive gaps in knowledge regarding the distribution of animals, especially invertebrates. My research seeks to fill these gaps in knowledge by integrating conventional ecological methods, morphological taxonomy, and 'omics approaches for comprehensive biodiversity assessment.

## Highlighted Projects

  <summary>Studying marine invertebrate cryptofauna using Autonomous Reef Monitoring Structures (ARMS)</summary>

  <p>The small invertebrate cryptofauna and epifauna are typically overlooked yet comprise most marine biodiversity at reef habtiats and provide a vital trophic link to natural resources like commercially important fisheries. My colleagues and I at Lehigh University and the Smithsonian National Museum of Natural History use Autonomous Reef Monitoring Structures to provide the first assessments of cryptofauna biodiversity in the offshore Gulf and to determine environment controls on cryptofaunal assemblages. </p>

  <p>Read about this work in our recent publication in <a href="https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/sciadv.aeg9582">Science Advances</a> and read about <a href="https://naturalhistory.si.edu/research/global-arms-program">ARMS</a></p>
</details>

  <summary>Elucidating the distributions of deep-sea species through integrative 'omics analysis</summary>

  <div class="research-card">
    <img src="{{ '/assets/img/pr_tree.jpg' | relative_url }}" alt="Phylogenetic tree">
    <div class="research-overlay">
      <div class="research-text"></div>
    </div>
  </div>

  <p>The distributions of deep-sea species are poorly mapped, many cryptic species exist, and cosmopolitan species may or may not represent connect populations. Exploration efforts to map and identify species and their distributions that integrate 'omics and conventional approaches are necessary to map biodiversity before it is lost. My work focuses on deep-sea coral communities at biodiversity hotspots like seamounts, submarine canyons, and chemosynthetic ecosystems where communities of benthic invertebrates and fish thrive. By combining collections, genomic analysis, and video annotation, we are improving our understanding of important deep-sea species</p>

  <p>Read about this work in <a href="https://doi.org/10.1111/1755-0998.70130">Molecular Ecology Resources</a> and <a href="https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0967063723001954?via%3Dihub">Deep-sea Research Part I</a></p>
</details>

  <p>Species interactions and interaction networks are the currency of biodiversity and the foundation of ecosystem services. My early research focused on invasive species and mutualisms in plant-insect interaction in forests and modified old field habitats. My independent research found that in the absence of an irruptive herbivore, ant-hemipteran mutualisms on goldenrod do not prevent this common wildflower from producing robust inflorescences and setting seed.</p>
