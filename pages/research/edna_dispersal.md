Fundamental to the effectiveness of eDNA sequencing for biodiversity assessment is the spatiotemporal comprehensiveness of environmental samples. eDNA sampling may capture exogenous DNA from organisms outside of the study area. My PhD dissertation research focused on understanding how laboratory protocols and local environmental conditions, namely temperature, affect the spatiotemporal resolution of these data.

  <p> My first project as a PhD student used an experimental approach to determine the persistence of eDNA in ocean microcosms that reflected combinations of temperature, dissolved oxygen concentration, and pH found naturally in the offshore, subtropical ocean. I found that eDNA degradation was strongly controlled by temperature, but weakly by dissolved oxygen concentration and pH. This work constrains the theory regarding how long eDNA may persist in the ocean, a necessary step to predict its spatiotemporal distribution.</p>

  <p>Read about this work in our publication in <a href="https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.est.2c01672">Environmental Science and Technology</a></p>
